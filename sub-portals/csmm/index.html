Trustworthy Motion Metrics

Two additional SONAIR evidence metrics compare the real TCP movement with an estimated ideal one-step command trajectory. They focus on both precision and recovery after deviation.

Estimating ideal trajectory from command velocities...

Ideal vs Actual Position Inference

The dataset does not provide a direct target_position field, so the ideal next position is inferred from the current command speed and the previous real TCP position.

  • Actual next positionRead x, y, z directly from the TCP log at the next sample.
  • Command speedRead vx, vy, vz from speedl xd values.
  • Ideal next positionPrevious actual position + command speed * time gap.
  • Tracking errorDistance between actual next position and ideal next position.
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Actual next} &= [x_i, y_i, z_i] \\ \text{Command speed} &= [v_x, v_y, v_z] \\ \text{Ideal next} &= \text{Previous actual} + \text{Command speed} \times \Delta t \\ \text{Tracking error} &= \mathrm{distance}(\text{Actual next}, \text{Ideal next}) \end{aligned} \]

Two Motion Quality Metrics

The first metric shows how often the robot stays within a tolerance. The second shows whether above-limit errors recover quickly.

  • Threshold-based Movement AccuracyAt a chosen millimetre threshold, count how many samples stay inside the limit, then divide by all samples.
  • Recovery-aware Error TimelineStart from a perfect score of 1, then subtract penalties for average error size, above-limit samples, and long repeated error runs.
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Threshold Accuracy} &= \frac{\text{inside-limit samples}}{\text{all samples}} \\ A &= \text{average error penalty} \\ B &= \text{above-limit rate} \\ C &= \text{long-run penalty} \\ \text{Recovery Score} &= 1 - (0.4A + 0.3B + 0.3C) \end{aligned} \]
